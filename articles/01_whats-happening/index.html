What's Happening in Palestine?

For some, October 7, 2023, marks the beginning of the latest war on Gaza. For Palestinians, this date is not a beginning, but a brutal intensification of a catastrophe that has been unfolding for three quarters of a century. To understand the genocide being inflicted upon Palestine today, one must look not to the events of a single day, but to the maps drawn in 1948, the mass expulsions, and the creation of a refugee population that has been trapped and targeted ever since. The current destruction is the latest and most lethal chapter of the Nakba, a process of ethnic cleansing that began with the creation of Israel and has never stopped.

Some background is essential to understanding how this oppression reached its current, genocidal phase. Following World War I, the League of Nations granted Britain the mandate to govern Palestine. This period was critical because the mandate incorporated the Balfour Declaration's promise of a national home for the Jewish people while referring to the indigenous Arab majority merely as non-Jewish communities with civil and religious rights, but no national political recognition. This created a fundamental and legally sanctioned imbalance, prioritizing a foreign settler population over the native inhabitants. For the Palestinians, this influx, backed by a colonial mandate, was an existential threat to their dream of self-determination on their own land.

When the conflict started in its modern and most consequential form was in 1947 and 1948. As the British mandate ended, the United Nations proposed Resolution 181, a plan to partition Palestine into separate Arab and Jewish states. The plan was fundamentally unjust, granting the Zionist settlers, who owned only a small fraction of the land and made up one third of the population, over half of the territory. The Palestinian people and the surrounding Arab nations rejected this theft of their homeland. The war that followed was not a spontaneous clash, but a planned and systematic campaign by Zionist militias to cleanse the land.

How it started was through a premeditated ethnic cleansing operation. Renowned Israeli historian Ilan Pappé documents how Zionist forces implemented Plan Dalet starting in early 1947. This plan was designed not just to win a war, but to systematically depopulate the land of its native population to make way for an exclusivist Jewish state. Over 750,000 Palestinians were terrorized, expelled, or fled from their homes in what they call the Nakba, or catastrophe. Entire villages were destroyed, and well documented massacres like the one at Deir Yassin became tools of psychological warfare to ensure the mass flight of the population. By the time the fighting ended, the new state of Israel had been established on 78 percent of the land, and a Palestinian refugee population had been created, one that exists to this day.

The events of October 7 were a dramatic and unprecedented operation by the Palestinian resistance, breaching the walls of the open air prison that is Gaza. This operation, led by Hamas and other resistance factions, targeted Israeli military installations and settlements, which are themselves illegal under international law. While the chaos of any military action is tragic, this was an act of armed resistance by a captive people against their occupier. The operation was a direct response to the relentless violence of the Israeli occupation: the daily raids in the West Bank, the killings, the administrative detention of thousands without charge, the desecration of Al Aqsa Mosque, and the slow, agonizing death of the siege on Gaza. However, to frame the subsequent genocide as a mere response to October 7 is to intentionally ignore the context of 75 years of ethnic cleansing, occupation, siege, and dispossession. Israel has occupied the West Bank, Gaza, and East Jerusalem since 1967, and has maintained a crippling blockade on Gaza since 2007, creating what many have called an open air prison for two million people.

In response to this blow to its military prestige, Israel launched a genocidal campaign of unprecedented and barbaric scale. Within months, the death toll in Gaza surpassed 70,000 according to Palestinian health authorities, with thousands more buried under the rubble. The overwhelming majority of the dead are women and children, targeted in their homes, schools, and hospitals. The International Court of Justice was seized of the matter, with South Africa filing a case charging Israel with violating the Genocide Convention. In January 2024, the ICJ issued a landmark ruling, finding it plausible that Israel's acts could amount to genocide and ordering it to take measures to prevent genocidal acts. Human rights organizations have since concluded that the evidence, including the deliberate blocking of food, water, and medicine, the systematic destruction of all of Gaza's universities, the targeting of hospitals and refugee camps, and the explicit use of dehumanizing genocidal language by Israeli officials and soldiers, points to a calculated plan to bring about the physical destruction of Palestinians in Gaza. The Zionist entity has shown itself to be not a democracy, but a violent, expansionist colonial project willing to commit mass slaughter to achieve its goals.

Even the so called ceasefires have offered little respite and have become a grotesque part of the cycle of death and deception. Temporary truces are brokered, only to be systematically violated by Israeli forces. By February 2026, official records documented over 1,500 Israeli violations of the latest ceasefire agreement, resulting in hundreds more martyrs. These attacks have continued while the international community, led by the United States, Israel's primary enabler and supplier of weapons, insists the ceasefire is still holding. This proves that for Palestinians, a ceasefire is just a euphemism for a slower, but still lethal, rate of killing, and that the so called rules based international order is a lie when it comes to Palestinian lives.

The world watches as bombs fall on refugee camps whose inhabitants have already been displaced for 75 years, as families are starved by siege, and as international court rulings are ignored. From the ethnic cleansing of 1948, through decades of brutal occupation, to the genocidal violence of today, the thread is unbroken. The only way to understand the horror of the present is to recognize Palestinian resistance as a legitimate struggle for freedom and to see the Zionist project for what it has always been: a political ideology and state project sustained by displacement, occupation, and military force, not targeting Jews as a people. Criticism here is directed at Zionism and Israeli state actions, not religion or ethnicity.
